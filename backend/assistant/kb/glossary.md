## Common terms
- Abstract: a short summary at the top of a paper.
- Preprint: a paper shared before peer review (arXiv is the best-known preprint server).
- Peer review: experts check a paper before a journal publishes it. It reduces but does not remove errors.
- DOI: a permanent identifier for a published paper, like 10.1038/nature14539.
- arXiv id: the identifier of a preprint, like 2301.01234.
- PMID: the PubMed identifier of a biomedical paper.
- Citation: a reference from one paper to another; citation counts show influence, not correctness.
- Open access: free to read legally. Only open-access full text can be analysed in depth.
- Methodology: how the study was done. Limitations: what the authors say the study cannot show.
- Literature review: a survey of what is already known on a topic.

## How a beginner can start
Pick one topic you care about. Read the abstract, introduction and conclusion of a recent review paper first. Search for it in shoRDs, open it, run the cited analysis to see the main statements and their source sentences, then read those sentences in the paper. Note unfamiliar terms and look them up. If you want guidance from an experienced person, browse the Mentors tab.
