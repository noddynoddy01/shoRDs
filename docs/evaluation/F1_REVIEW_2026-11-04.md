# F1 review: the evidence (claim) guard

Date: 2026-11-04. Scope: `backend/llm/claimGuard.js`, its use in `backend/llm/intelligence.js` (INTEL_PROMPT_VERSION `2026.3-intel`), the regression fixture and the limits register. **F1 has not been validated against real-world summary accuracy.** Nothing here is a measured error rate; the cases are small, hand-written and fictional, and several were chosen because they are hard.

## What F1 does
After the model answers, each statement is checked against only the passages it cites, one statement at a time (a failing statement is removed; the others stay). A statement is **removed** when: a number in it does not appear in the cited passages (`STATEMENT_NUMBER_NOT_IN_EVIDENCE`); it uses a strong overclaim word that the passages lack (`OVERCLAIM`); it states a direction opposite to the passage's direction word, or its negation does not match (`REVERSED_MEANING`); or the passages share too little vocabulary with it (`WEAK_SUPPORT`). It is **kept but flagged** when the passage hedges or limits the claim and the statement does not (`HEDGE_DROPPED`, `SCOPE_LIMIT_DROPPED`). Kept statements carry `evidenceSpans`, the sentences that best match, so a reader can look.
It is a set of fixed lexical rules. It does not understand meaning.

## Protections preserved (verified by running tests, 2026-11-04)
- `INTEL_PROMPT_VERSION` `2026.3-intel` and `PROMPT_VERSION` `2026.3` are part of every cache key; `cacheIdentityAudit.test.js` pins a fingerprint of the system prompt, guard limits and tag list, so changing any of them without a version bump fails the build. Older cached analyses (made before the guard) can no longer be served.
- The guard was **not loosened**. Every Phase 3 change adds tests or documentation; the limits register records current behaviour and fails if a change flips a case, forcing a version bump and a register update.
- Pathological input (long runs of digits, dots, commas, brackets): 21 statements checked in about 20 ms, bounded by a test.

## The regression fixture (39 tests: 37 cases + fixture check + summary)
Synthetic papers. Categories: faithful 13, numbers 7, reversals 4 (cued), uncued reversals 2, overclaim 2, hedge 2, scope 1, wrong attribution 1, unsupported 1, quantitative results 4. All cases behave as recorded. Known misses inside it: `m01` (reversal with a verb not in the direction list), `m02` (reversal visible only in the order of numbers), `m03` (a number that belongs to a different system in the same passage).

## The limits register (22 more cases, `evidence_limits_register.json`, run by `evidenceLimits.test.js`)
Built to probe what the guard cannot see. Counts are over hand-built adversarial cases and are **not rates**.

| Group | Cases | Result today |
|---|---|---|
| Wrong attribution (claim given to the wrong method, dataset or author) | W01-W04 | all 4 kept: unfaithful statements pass |
| Paraphrased numbers (same quantity in other words or units) | P01-P03 | P01 kept (unfaithful); P03 dropped (correct); P02 kept (faithful, correct) |
| Subtle reversals (meaning flipped without a listed cue word) | R01-R05 | R01-R04 kept; R05 dropped by a cue |
| Context-dependent claims (true in one condition or population, stated generally) | X01-X06 | all 6 kept |
| Faithful statements the guard drops | L01-L04 | all 4 dropped: L01 "improves error rate by 20%" where the paper says "reduces" (lower is better, so the paraphrase is faithful but reads as opposite direction); L02 "Experiment 5" vs "Experiment No. 5" and L03 "assuming i.i.d. samples" vs "Without loss of generality we assume i.i.d." (negation/abbreviation handling, `REVERSED_MEANING`); L04 "0.874" vs "87.4 percent" (a unit/scale change, `STATEMENT_NUMBER_NOT_IN_EVIDENCE`) |

Totals: of 17 unfaithful statements, 15 kept; of 5 faithful statements, 4 dropped. **Both errors are real and the guard is biased towards keeping.** An unfaithful statement that survives looks exactly like a good one.

## Failure patterns, in plain words
1. **Wrong attribution** passes because every number and word exists somewhere in the cited passages.
2. **Paraphrase and unit changes** pass or fail depending on whether the digits match exactly; "twice" or "a 12-point gain" are not compared with numbers.
3. **Reversals without a cue word** pass (the direction list is finite).
4. **Context** ("on the validation split only", "in mice") is not compared beyond the flagged hedge and scope words.
5. **False drops** happen with faithful paraphrase: a metric where lower is better described as "improves" instead of "reduces", abbreviations and dots ("No.", "i.i.d."), and a fraction written as a percentage. This costs recall; it is the safe direction, and it is why the guard must not be tightened further without measurement either.
6. Three number tokenisers exist in the backend (`numbersIn`, `guardNumbers`, the device brief's), with small differences. Not a bug that was demonstrated, but a maintenance risk (see defect M5).

## What may and may not be said
- Say: "statements that fail automatic checks are removed; the sentences behind kept statements can be inspected".
- Do not say: verified, accurate, fact-checked, reliable. The API field `confidence` is a heuristic over counts of kept statements, not a probability of correctness; the UI must never show it.
- Every response and screen that shows kept statements should carry the standing caution (`EVIDENCE_COPY.checkNote`).

## What would validate F1 (not done)
The review kit (`docs/evaluation/F1_REVIEW_KIT.md`): 5 to 10 CC BY papers, two independent readers, blinded sheets, miss rate and false-drop rate with confidence intervals. Two limits to know first: results of a few papers have very wide intervals; and the product does not store the model's raw output, so false drops cannot be measured until an opt-in capture exists (owner decision D-F1-1).

## Recommendation
Merge-worthy as a conservative safety layer **provided** the product wording stays at "passed automatic checks". Not a basis for any accuracy claim.
