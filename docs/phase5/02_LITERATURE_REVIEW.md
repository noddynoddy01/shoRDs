## 2.1 What is, and is not, knowable about Claude

**Unknown and not pursued:** Claude's architecture, training data, training procedure and system prompts. This engine does not try to reproduce them, and nothing in this phase copies, scrapes or trains on Claude output (see `07_FINETUNING_FEASIBILITY.md`).

**Learnable from observable behaviour (our own production traffic, within our terms with the provider):** what a good brief contains and how it is organised; which fields researchers need; what kinds of error a strong model makes on papers (wrong numbers, dropped hedges, reversed results, unsupported conclusions); how long and how costly a good answer is. The Phase 4 benchmark with raw capture is the instrument for this: it records *our* prompts, *our* chunks and the model's reply for papers whose licence we confirmed. What it can establish is a **quality bar and an error taxonomy**; it cannot establish why the model behaves as it does.

**Public techniques that can reach similar research-specific outcomes** (not "the same as Claude"): structured extraction before summarisation, hierarchical merging for long documents, retrieval of the relevant passages per question, quote-anchored citations, and separate verification. Whether they match Claude on shoRDs' papers is an empirical question: **NOT RUN** (`05_EVALUATION_FRAMEWORK_AND_BENCHMARK_STATUS.md`).

## 2.2 The ten topics and what each means for the engine

| Topic | Evidence | Consequence in the design | State |
|---|---|---|---|
| 1. Scientific document understanding | PDFs lose structure on extraction (columns, tables, equations). Layout-aware tools exist (GROBID, Nougat◇, Docling◇, PyMuPDF◇) | Stage A keeps section and page provenance, **flags** what was not preserved (equations, table layout, figures), and refuses to guess. A real PDF extractor service is needed for production | Stage A IMPLEMENTED for JATS/HTML and for pdftotext output; layout-aware extractor PROPOSED; real-PDF behaviour NOT RUN |
| 2. Long-context modelling | **RULER ✔ (arXiv 2404.06654):** advertised context length far exceeds the length at which models actually hold up; only about half of 17 tested models kept quality at 32K. **Lost in the Middle ✔ (2307.03172):** accuracy is highest when the relevant information is at the start or end of the context | Do not rely on a model's headline context window. The pipeline never sends more than about 9,000 characters per field call or 8,000 per section call, and puts the instruction first. Context claims of candidate models (256K to 1M) are **not** treated as usable for our task until measured | IMPLEMENTED (design); quality at length NOT RUN |
| 3. Hierarchical summarisation | **BooookScore ✔ (2310.00785):** compares *hierarchical merging* with *incremental updating* on book-length text using 1,193 human annotations of eight coherence-error types; incremental updating scored lower on coherence but kept more detail | Stage C summarises section groups, then merges (a two-level tree). Long sections are merged; short ones are single calls. Merge fidelity is a known risk, so merged claims must still anchor to an original chunk | IMPLEMENTED; effect on quality NOT RUN |
| 4. Retrieval-augmented generation | RAG (Lewis et al.)◇ | Stage B retrieves candidate chunks per field (section priority and cue words) instead of sending the whole paper for every field | IMPLEMENTED (rule-based retrieval; embedding retrieval PROPOSED) |
| 5. Extractive vs abstractive | Extractive output is faithful but stiff; abstractive is fluent but can drift | Stage B is **extractive-anchored** (every item carries a verbatim quote that must exist in the chunk); Stage D may paraphrase but only from claims, and Stage E re-checks | IMPLEMENTED |
| 6. Evidence-grounded generation | Attribution and citation-verification work (for example FActScore◇) | Items cite `{chunkId, quote}`; unknown ids and quotes not found verbatim are dropped and counted | IMPLEMENTED, TESTED (fabricated ids/quotes in `nativePipeline.test.js`) |
| 7. Scientific claim extraction | **SciRIFF ✔ (2406.07835):** 137K instruction instances over 54 tasks (extraction, summarisation, QA, claim verification, classification); fine-tuning on it improved nine held-out tasks by 70.6% on average (relative figure from the abstract; licence of the data not stated there). SciFact◇, SciTLDR◇ are the claim-verification and extreme-summary benchmarks | Stage B is the claim-extraction stage. SciRIFF-style fine-tuning is the most relevant public route (see fine-tuning report), subject to checking its licence | Stage B IMPLEMENTED with a stub; quality NOT RUN |
| 8. Numerical and statistical result extraction | Numbers are the highest-cost error. Table-structure recovery is hard | Numbers are copied, never recomputed (prompt rule, plus the guard's whole-number match against the cited text). Table cells that lose their columns are flagged `TABLE_STRUCTURE_LOST` and every statement from such a chunk carries `TABLE_STRUCTURE_UNCERTAIN` | IMPLEMENTED, TESTED on a fictional PDF |
| 9. Contradiction and hallucination detection | **MiniCheck ✔ (2404.10774):** a 770M-parameter fact-checker matched GPT-4-level accuracy on LLM-AggreFact at about 400x lower cost; its training data was synthesised with GPT-4 (a provenance and terms question for us). AlignScore◇, SummaC◇, QAFactEval◇ are earlier factual-consistency scorers | A small learned checker could later complement the lexical claim guard. It would be an *additional* gate, never a replacement. The guard's **gaps** found in this phase are in `08_SECURITY_AND_LICENSING_REVIEW.md` (F-5-1, F-5-2) | Lexical guard INTEGRATED; learned checker PROPOSED |
| 10. Long-document compression and retention | The BooookScore trade-off above (coherence vs detail) | The representation keeps *all* anchored claims, de-duplicated, and the brief picks from them; compression happens at the brief, where it is visible and bounded (a maximum number of statements per section) | IMPLEMENTED |

## 2.3 Design consequences stated plainly

1. **The language model is one replaceable component.** Seven of nine stages are rules or checks; the model is called for extraction, section summaries and the final wording only.
2. **A model's fluency is not evidence.** Every statement must point to a passage and survive an automatic check, and the check is described as a check, not as proof.
3. **Multi-stage costs more calls.** The pipeline makes about 20 to 25 model calls per paper where the current production path makes one. Whether the extra structure improves human-rated quality enough to justify that cost is the central empirical question of the evaluation. It is not assumed.
4. **Everything quality-related is unmeasured.** No literature result above is a measurement of shoRDs, of any candidate model on our papers, or of Claude.
